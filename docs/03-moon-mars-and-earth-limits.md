# Moon, Mars, and what Earth cannot copy

This file fixes the two targets for the weekend and the limits every dossier must include. Numbers below are order-of-magnitude briefing values so the team shares one picture. The project must replace them with values read from the datasets we actually open, and it must cite those datasets. Do not present this page as a measurement.

## Why the south pole of the Moon

A permanent base is not the same problem as a short visit to a flat mare. Apollo landed near the equator on relatively smooth lava plains. Those sites are well lit in a simple day–night cycle of about 14 Earth days of sun and 14 Earth days of night. They are a poor model for a base that must stay.

NASA’s Artemis planning has focused on the **south polar region**. The reasons are practical:

- Some crater rims and ridges get sun for a large fraction of the year. Sunlight means power and a milder thermal environment than the bottom of a crater.
- Nearby crater floors can be **permanently shadowed**. They are extremely cold. Water ice can sit there for a long time. Ice is a resource for water, oxygen, and fuel, if it can be reached and processed.
- The useful ground is often a **ridge or a crater rim**: sunlit, sloped, next to much colder shadow. That is a harder driving and building problem than a flat plain.
- Earth stays near the horizon from the poles, which shapes communication and navigation.

Candidate areas discussed in public NASA south-pole planning include regions around Shackleton, Haworth, Shoemaker, Faustini, de Gerlache, Nobile, and the connecting ridge between Shackleton and de Gerlache. The weekend project should **name one** of these, or a clearly defined point on a rim, and read its slope and context from LRO or a Trek layer. Do not average the whole south pole into one number.

### What we will try to match

| Trait of a south-pole ridge base | Earth quantity we can screen | What the rehearsal is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sloped rim or ridge, not a cliff and not a plain | Slope from a digital elevation model | Wheel tests, walking, antenna and solar placement |
| Almost no vegetation, loose rocky or dusty ground | A vegetation index near zero, plus a bare-ground check in imagery | Mobility and instrument tests on barren ground |
| Large temperature swings and real cold | Land-surface temperature | Thermal procedures, batteries, night operations. Earth cold is only a partial stand-in |
| Ice or permafrost within a practical distance | Snow, ice, or permafrost maps near the site | Sampling and drilling practice, not a copy of a lunar cold trap |
| Dust and dry, wind-worked ground | Aerosol or dust products, and dry climate data | Seals, solar panels, cameras |
| Long sight lines and harsh light | High-elevation or high-latitude sites, checked in imagery | Navigation and field-operations practice |

### What we will not pretend to match

See the limits section below. In particular, a permanently shadowed lunar crater can be far colder than any outdoor place on Earth. No Earth site is a thermal analog for that floor. A nearby Earth ice body can still be an **operations** analog for “go sample the ice,” which is a different claim. Write the claim at the right strength.

## Why one Mars case, not all of Mars

Mars is a whole planet with lava plains, ancient lake basins, polar ice caps, dunes, canyons, and caves. A weekend project that tries to analog all of them will describe none of them well.

Pick **one** Mars problem and say so on the first slide. The default is to choose while opening the data, using this rule:

- If the Earth screen is already built around cold, dry, barren, sloped ground, pair it with a **ground-ice** Mars region, such as a mid-latitude icy terrain. The shared question is: how do you work on cold ground where ice is in or under the surface?
- If the stronger Earth signal is wind, dust, and salts, pair it with a **dusty sedimentary** Mars region instead. The shared question is: how do dust and salts load machines and change the surface?

Name the Mars place. Public examples teams already use include Jezero crater, Gale crater, Arcadia Planitia, and parts of Valles Marineris. Prefer a place you can point to in Mars Trek (CTX imagery and MOLA topography) so the judge sees a target, not a globe.

Useful Mars traits to put in the dossier, only where data exists:

- Slope and roughness at the scale of a traverse, not only the scale of a continent.
- Minerals that matter for the case: clays, sulfates, or other alteration products where a CRISM or published map covers the site.
- Ice or hydrated minerals if that is the case you picked.
- Dust activity if that is the case you picked.

Leave a cell blank when the dataset does not cover it. A blank cell with the words “no data” is more credible than a guessed number.

## Earth cannot be the Moon or Mars

Put this table, in shortened form, in the dossier and on one slide. It is not a disclaimer to hide. It is the scientific result.

| Condition | Moon | Mars | Earth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gravity | About 1/6 of Earth | About 3/8 of Earth | The only full-gravity outdoor test we have. Wheel loads, dust lofting, and human gait will differ |
| Atmosphere | Essentially a vacuum | Thin carbon-dioxide air, roughly 1 percent of Earth’s surface pressure, varying by season and height | Full air. No outdoor vacuum test. Parachutes, seals, and sound do not transfer |
| Radiation | No magnetic shield and no air. Surface dose is a design driver | Thin air, so the surface dose is still high | Air and magnetic field block most of it. Outdoor Earth tests do not qualify electronics or crews for that dose |
| Temperature | Equatorial day–night swings of hundreds of degrees. Shadowed polar floors can fall below about 100 K, colder than any outdoor Earth site | Wide swings, often far below freezing at night, milder where the air and ground allow | Earth can be hot or cold. It cannot reproduce lunar vacuum temperatures or the coldest shadowed floors |
| Sky and time | About 14 Earth days of sun, then about 14 of night, except near some polar peaks | A sol is about 24.6 hours. Global dust storms can dim the sun for weeks | A 24-hour day and a thick, wetter atmosphere. Polar night is a partial light analog only |
| Life | No known surface life | No confirmed surface life. The search is a reason to go | Life is everywhere, including “empty” deserts. A biosignature test on Earth must account for contamination |
| Water | Ice mainly in cold traps and in some minerals. Liquid water is not stable on the surface | Ice at the poles and in the subsurface at mid-latitudes. Liquid water is not stable on the surface today | Liquid water, rain, and biology alter the ground even in dry regions |

Reduced gravity, vacuum, thin air, and radiation are laboratory and simulation problems. The Earth analog is for the pieces that **do** transfer: terrain, materials, dust behavior in a qualitative sense, ice-handling technique, navigation in barren ground, and team operations.

## How to talk about a site without over-claiming

Use this pattern in every dossier.

**Matches.** Name the dimension, the Earth value, the Moon or Mars value, the unit, and the source. Example shape: “Median slope inside the 2 km patch is 12 degrees (SRTM). The named lunar rim patch is 9–15 degrees (LOLA). This is close enough for a mobility rehearsal.”

**Breaks.** Name the dimension and why the analog stops. Example shape: “Night temperature here reaches about −20 °C. A shadowed lunar floor is far colder, and it sits in vacuum. This site does not test radiator design for that floor.”

**How a mission would use it.** One concrete rehearsal. Example shape: “Drive a rover traverse that climbs a 10–15 degree barren slope, then sample a nearby ice or permafrost exposure, and log power and wheel current. Do the vacuum, radiation, and suit tests in a chamber, not in this valley.”

If the screen’s top hit is a famous site, keep it, and say so. Fame is not a flaw. Hiding it is. The comparison site still belongs in the project so the reader can see the method rather than a single favorite pin.

## Default pair for this team

Unless the first data pass shows a clearly better pair, lock this:

1. **Moon:** one named Artemis south-pole ridge or rim region, treated as an operations analog (slope, barren ground, cold, ice nearby, dust).
2. **Mars:** one named ground-ice or dust-and-salts region, treated as a process analog.
3. **Earth result:** the best less-famous cell the screen supports, compared with one famous control (Atacama, the Antarctic Dry Valleys, Haughton, Death Valley, Iceland, or Hawaii) scored the same way.
4. **Always stated:** gravity, vacuum or thin air, and radiation are out of scope for the outdoor analog.
