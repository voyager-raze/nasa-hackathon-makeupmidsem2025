## What we are doing

We will not hunt for one place on Earth that “looks like” the Moon or Mars. We will pick two real exploration targets, turn their useful traits into numbers Earth satellites can measure, screen the planet, and write an honest dossier: what matches, how well, what does not, and how a mission team could use the Earth site before flight.

The default project is a **partial-match finder**:

1. An Artemis-style south-pole ridge base on the Moon (slope, barren ground, extreme cold, ice nearby, dust).
2. One Mars case: ground ice, or dust and salts.
3. A global coarse screen, then one less-famous Earth hit plus one famous site as a comparison.
4. A simple map, a seven-slide demo, a public repo, and the Space Apps form.

Earth cannot copy lunar gravity, vacuum, radiation, or the thin Martian air. Saying that is part of the answer.
