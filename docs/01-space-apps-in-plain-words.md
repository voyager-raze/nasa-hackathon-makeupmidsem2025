# NASA Space Apps in plain words

## What it is

NASA Space Apps is a two-day global hackathon. Anyone can join. NASA writes a set of challenges. Each team picks one and builds something with open data from NASA or a partner space agency.

A project can be a map, a small web tool, a notebook, a written report, a story, or another clear way to show the work. The point is to use real data to answer the challenge, then explain the answer so a judge can follow it in a few minutes.

This year’s event is **14–15 November 2026**. Registration can stay open until the hackathon closes on 15 November. This team is on the **Delhi** local event, under the name **Mid-Sem Make Up**, on the Moon-and-Mars analog challenge.

## How the weekend works

1. Register on [spaceappschallenge.org](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/).
2. Join a local event, or the online Universal Event.
3. Form a team of no more than six people.
4. Pick one official challenge.
5. Build during the weekend. The core of the project should come from these two days.
6. Submit on the team’s **Project** tab.

The Project tab opened on Saturday, 14 November at 9:00 am, and it closes on **Sunday, 15 November at 11:59 pm local time**. For this team, that is 11:59 pm in Delhi. After that, the page is for judging, not for last-minute edits.

Submit even if the project is small. A submitted project is what earns a participant certificate and what can be judged. An unfinished idea that never gets submitted cannot.

## Submitted, nominated, and winner

These are different steps. Do not treat them as the same thing.

- **Submitted.** The team filled the project form before the deadline and checked that it should be judged. Everyone who submits a complete project can receive a participant certificate.
- **Global nominee.** After the weekend, local-event judges and Universal Event judges pick projects to send forward. Delhi projects are compared with other projects in that judging path. Universal Event projects are compared with other Universal Event projects. The chance of being nominated is meant to be similar on both paths.
- **Global finalist and honorable mention.** NASA and partner experts review the nominees. These names are expected in December 2026.
- **Global winner.** A smaller executive group picks the global winners. Those names are expected in January 2027. In recent years there have been ten global awards, such as best use of science, best use of data, best use of technology, and best mission concept.

A clear, honest, data-backed project is what moves a team from “submitted” toward “nominated.” A pretty idea with no data, no public link, or a broken demo usually stops at submission.

## How judges look at a project

Judges use five questions. They carry similar weight. A project that is brilliant on science and confusing to watch still loses points. A beautiful slide deck that never uses NASA data also loses points.

| Criterion | The question a judge is asking |
| --- | --- |
| Impact | Who is this for, and how much would it help them? |
| Creativity | Is there a fresh idea, or is this a copy of a well-known site and a well-known method? |
| Validity | Are the numbers, sources, and limits real? Could someone else check the claim? |
| Relevance | Does this answer **this** challenge, with NASA or partner data in the middle of the solution? |
| Presentation | Can a stranger understand the story, the demo, and the result quickly? |

For this challenge, a useful reader is a scientist or engineer who picks Earth sites to test instruments, robots, or field methods before a Moon or Mars mission. The project should help that person see a match, see the gaps, and see a next test.

## What the project page must include

Write the project page in **English**. A demo video in another language needs English subtitles.

The usual required pieces are:

- A short project name.
- A high-level summary: what you built, which part of the challenge it answers, and why it matters.
- A **project demo** link: a slide deck of **at most 7 slides** (title slide included), or a video of **at most 30 seconds**.
- A **final project** link: the real product (site, repo, report, or files).
- Project details: how it works, what you set out to do, and what the result is.
- A **Super Intelligence (SI)** note. NASA’s 2026 materials use that term for tools that generate text, code, images, or decisions. Name the tools and what they did. If none were used, say so.
- **NASA data and partner data:** the name and link of each dataset. At least one must be used in the solution, not only mentioned.
- **References:** libraries, articles, images, music, and tools.

Two confirmation boxes matter: you agree to the rules, and the work is the team’s, with outside sources listed.

## Rules that quietly eliminate teams

- The demo link and the project link must open for a stranger. Test each link in a private browser window. “Anyone with the link” is the right sharing setting.
- Citing a NASA fact or pasting a NASA photo is not the same as using a NASA dataset. The data has to do work in the project.
- For global-award eligibility, use at least one data source from the **Resources** tab of this challenge. Extra datasets are welcome on top of that.
- Do not put NASA logos, the NASA flag, or mission insignia into generated images or video. Unaltered NASA photos, with credit, are a different case. Follow the image rules and name the source.
- If an image or video is generated, put a visible watermark on it that says it was generated and names the tool.
- Keep the team at six people or fewer, and make sure every member is registered on the team page.
- No profanity on the project page.
- Open-source code is fine. Credit it.

## What “good” looks like on this weekend

Judges see thousands of projects. The ones that travel further usually do five things:

1. Answer the written challenge in the first paragraph.
2. Use a real dataset in a way the judge can see.
3. Show a result, not only a plan.
4. Admit what the method cannot do.
5. Make the demo short enough to finish.

The rest of this folder turns those five habits into a specific project for the analog challenge.
